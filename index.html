What News Move Stock Prices? Evidence from a Century of Subjective Expectations

with Aaron Huberman

Survey-based measures of subjective cash-flow expectations exist only for the last four decades, which limits what can be said about the historical drivers of stock prices. We recover analyst-style subjective earnings expectations from the historical financial-news record, calibrated to modern survey data, and extend the subjective Campbell-Shiller decomposition of price ratios back through the twentieth century. This lets us ask which news, about cash flows or about discount rates, moved prices across the Great Depression, the postwar period, and the Great Inflation, and how the answer compares with the modern survey era.

Volatility Expectations and the Pricing Kernel

with Aaron Huberman · work in progress

We document that the curvature of the conditional pricing kernel estimated from S&P 500 index options falls by roughly half after 2008, and we link this break to a shift in investors' volatility and tail beliefs measured from survey data. We develop an Epstein-Zin economy with variance-biased, persistent beliefs to account for the pre- and post-2008 shapes of the kernel.
