abstract: >-
  Survey-based measures of subjective cash-flow expectations exist only for the last
  four decades, which limits what can be said about the historical drivers of stock prices.
  We recover analyst-style subjective earnings expectations from the historical financial-news
  record, calibrated to modern survey data, and extend the subjective Campbell-Shiller
  decomposition of price ratios back through the twentieth century. This lets us ask which news,
  about cash flows or about discount rates, moved prices across the Great Depression, the postwar
  period, and the Great Inflation, and how the answer compares with the modern survey era.
